<objective>
Build the table the October sync is actually about (EPA-D03): the head coach's "Scoring Probability
by Situation" method and our model, side by side, on the same axes, with sample sizes everywhere.

Four columns per cell, all keyed on `(down, distance_bin, field_half)`:

1. **His published number** — from `data/reference/hc_sp_tables/` (M3-02-03), with his own
   `Sample Size by D&D` count next to it.
2. **His rows, recomputed** — the same empirical scoring probability computed by us from HIS rows
   now in our canonical corpus, with our n. Where this differs from (1), the difference is itself a
   finding: same data, same question, two computations.
3. **Our non-head-coach rows** — the same empirical number from the rest of the corpus, with its n.
4. **The model** — mean out-of-fold expected points per cell from `oof_predictions_ep.parquet`,
   which M3-02-05's with-head-coach arm just rewrote. Out-of-fold matters: a cell must not be scored
   by a model that saw that game while fitting.

What this plan deliberately does NOT do: reproduce his `Reg` tab. M3-02-RESEARCH section 4.4 read
its formula cells and found hardcoded polynomials of rising degree per down and field half — the
signature of hand-transcribed Excel chart trendlines, not a fitted model. Reproducing that would be
reproducing the thing his own pitch ("professioneller und nachhaltiger gestalten") asks to move past.
His `Reg` values are already snapshotted in `reg_formulas.csv` and can be quoted; they are not
recomputed.

The honesty machinery is not optional decoration. Several of his own-half cells at distance >= 10
carry n in the low teens next to opposite-half cells with n in the hundreds for the same
(down, distance). Without n on every column, his estimates and the model's look equally confident,
and the argument M3-02-07 has to make disappears.

Purpose: HC-03's second half — the tabular comparison.
Output: `src/flag_football_ep/reports/hc_comparison.py`, `tests/test_reports_hc_comparison.py`,
`scripts/epa_comparison.py` and three CSVs under `data/reference/epa_refinement/`.
</objective>

  `*group_cols, label, n, successes, rate, ci_low, ci_high, muted` (Clopper-Pearson, `muted` is

- `prepare_ep_data(df)` produces `Next_Score_Half` (values `Touchdown`, `Opp_Touchdown`, `Safety`,
  `Opp_Safety`, `No_Score`), plus `half_end`, `game_end`, `scoring_event`, `drive_score_half`.
- `add_ep_variables` defines expected points as
  `0*No_Score_Prob + 2*Safety_Prob + 6*Touchdown_Prob - 2*Opp_Safety_Prob - 6*Opp_Touchdown_Prob`
  (`mutations.py:638-647`). Use exactly this weighting for the model column — do not invent a second
  one.

`model/evaluate.py::oof_frame` writes `data/processed/oof_predictions_ep.parquet` with columns
`game_id` (Utf8), `play_id` (Int32), `source` (Utf8) and the five `EP_PROB_LABELS` probability
columns in order: `Touchdown_Prob`, `Opp_Touchdown_Prob`, `Safety_Prob`, `Opp_Safety_Prob`,
`No_Score_Prob`. `(game_id, play_id)` is unique.

Canonical geometry (`docs/data-contract.md` line 82, `validation/checks.py::yardline_range`):
`yardline_50` is yards from the offense's OWN goal line, integer, 0..50 inclusive. Midfield is 25.

Head-coach snapshot schema from M3-02-03 (tidy):
`down, distance_bin, distance_bin_source, field_half, value` with `field_half` in `{own, opponent}`.
His uncluttered axis is distance 1..14 in whole yards with a final open bin; the clustered axis uses
`1-5`, `6-10`, `11-15`, `16-19`/`16-20`, `20`/`21-25`, `20+`/`25+` (own-half and opposite-half
columns use slightly different edges — do not force them onto one shared set of edges).

Spot values verified against the real workbook (M3-02-RESEARCH section 4.2), useful as sanity
anchors: down 1 / distance 1 / own half has SP 0.667 on n = 21; down 1 / distance 10 / own half has
n = 324.
