<objective>
Close METR-04 and propose METR-03.

**METR-04** needs no new measurement: `data/reference/baseline-methods/{summary,per_clip}.csv`
already proves the point. The challenge's declared threshold metric is the HUMAN pass rate -- and
it is `keine Review` for five of the six measured method/config rows, so it says nothing at all
about them. The continuous metric is defined for all six and ranks them
(mean fragments per expected player: GTA 0,264 | CBIoU baseline-matched 0,741 | BoT-SORT 0,836 |
ByteTrack baseline-matched 0,843 | ByteTrack defaults 0,979 | CBIoU defaults 1,077). The narrower
illustration inside the automatic view: ByteTrack defaults vs baseline-matched moves the automatic
threshold by two clips (55/61 -> 57/61) while the continuous metric moves from 0,979 to 0,843 and
the ideal-band fraction stays flat at 0/61 both times.

The trap this plan must not fall into: GTA looks best on the continuous metric and is exactly the
row `docs/baseline-messung.md` already warns about -- 0 splits, 364 merges, generic non-sport
embedding, median 12 crops per track, no human confirmation. An over-merge IMPROVES the continuous
metric while making identity worse. The caveat is repeated verbatim in the new section, not linked.

**METR-03** is a proposal, not a decision. Following the SoccerNet/SportsMOT pattern (one acceptance
metric, everything else directional/diagnostic), the proposal is: acceptance criterion = the human
pass rate (>= 90 % of the play without an identity switch), direction = the continuous metric,
diagnostic = the guard metric. The user decides; the plan ends in a blocking human-verify checkpoint
and the executor must not treat the wording as final before it.

Purpose: teams need to know what they are scored on, and the project needs a comparison that shows
the threshold metric's blind area without overselling the replacement.
Output: one new section in the measurement protocol, the wording in both challenge documents, drift
tests, and the user's decision on the record.
</objective>
