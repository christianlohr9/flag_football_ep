# Phase 2.0: Capture Protocol & Material Inventory - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-08-22
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Everything is in place to receive pilot-quality drone footage legally and reproducibly — before any CV code is written (REQ-S2-01). Four deliverables, all documents/reference data, no pipeline code: (1) a material inventory of existing footage per camera domain with 2–3 sample clips secured per domain, (2) a German one-page capture "Wunschzettel" for drone (primary) and elevated side camera (second domain), (3) a legal note recording how drone regulation and DSGVO consent are covered, (4) a sync convention that joins a video play to an existing Hudl-PBP play so Phase 2.3 can attach tracking to Strand-1 data.

**Critical framing from discussion:** The user does NOT fly the drone and has no authority over staff. The Videoanalyst or team staff operate the capture; the user is the recipient of whatever footage arrives ("ich muss mit dem arbeiten, was kommt"). Every deliverable is therefore written as a request-with-reasoning, never as a mandate, and every convention must work with zero cooperation from staff. Phase 2.0 must also complete without the Videoanalyst, who is currently unavailable.

Not this phase: the pilot pipeline itself (2.1: zero-shot, CVAT, RF-DETR, tracking, homography), snap detection (2.3), dataset buildout (2.2), any CV code.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Analyst strategy (unavailable Videoanalyst)
- **D-01 Unilateral draft + deferred ratification.** Same pattern as Phase 1.1: all four deliverables are drafted now by the user/agents; analyst-dependent items (inventory of footage the analyst holds, agreement to the Wunschzettel, Hudl-upload variant of the sync) carry an explicit `DEFERRED-ANALYST` block and are ratified when the analyst is back. The phase completes without the conversation having happened.
- **D-02 Capture is owned by staff/analyst, not the user.** The user owns no drone and will not operate one. Hardware is unknown → all recommendations are device-neutral (target values, not device settings).
- **D-03 Protocol = German "Wunschzettel", not a Pflichtenheft.** Tone: "so wäre es für die Analyse ideal, falls machbar" with a one-line reason per item. No "ihr müsst". Nothing in the doc assumes staff will follow it.
- **D-04 Tiered wish list: Ideal / Brauchbar / Unbrauchbar.** Every item (hover position & angle, altitude, resolution, frame rate, exposure, field coverage, recording duration vs battery swap, sync signal) is specified in three tiers so incoming material can be triaged immediately for Phase 2.1 pilot suitability. Locked targets from D-03 (project) feed the Ideal tier: fixed hover ~30–60 m, slightly oblique (not exactly top-down), 4K, fixed exposure, whole field in frame, one position per half-field drive, battery swap between drives (C-11).
- **D-05 Both domains in the Wunschzettel, drone first.** One page for the drone domain; a shorter block for the second domain (elevated tripod / side camera / Veo-class at official games where drones are banned, C-01) with the same three tiers. Official games otherwise never yield usable material for the 60/40 dataset mix (D-03 project).

### Deliverable artifacts
- **D-06 One document per deliverable in `docs/`**, German prose (code identifiers, column names and football vocabulary in English), matching `docs/data-contract.md` style:
  - `docs/capture-protocol.md` — the Wunschzettel (shareable with staff as-is)
  - `docs/material-inventory.md` — inventory explanation, domains, how clips are registered
  - `docs/capture-legal.md` — legal note (see D-12/D-13)
  - `docs/sync-convention.md` — video-play ↔ Hudl-PBP-play join
  Exact filenames are Claude's discretion as long as they follow the existing `docs/` naming style.
- **D-07 Inventory has a machine-readable companion:** a CSV under `data/reference/` (e.g. `video_inventory.csv`), one row per clip, following the maintained-CSV pattern of `team_mapping.csv` (comma, UTF-8 without BOM). Columns at minimum: domain, game/training identifier (`game_id` where a Hudl game exists), date, resolution, fps, duration, local path, content hash, notes. Phase 2.1 reads it to pick the pilot footage.
- **D-08 Sample clips live outside git.** Nothing binary in the repo; the CSV records the local path plus a content hash so clips can be re-located/verified. Where exactly (e.g. a gitignored `data/video/` following the `data/raw/hudl/*` ignore pattern, or an external folder) is Claude's discretion. Footage shows players → treat like the existing PII-bearing data (never committed).

### Sync convention
- **D-09 Staff is not asked to do anything for sync.** No required board/clap/timestamp overlay. A visible drive/board signal may appear in the Wunschzettel only as a wish with reasoning; the convention may not depend on it.
- **D-10 Reference side of the join is what already exists in Hudl:** the charted PBP (`PLAY #` → `play_id`, `ODK`, `DN`, `DIST`, `RESULT` …) and the per-play Hudl clips the analyst cuts anyway. The convention attaches video positions to existing Hudl plays; it never redefines plays. `DN`/`DIST`/`RESULT` serve as plausibility checks that the right play was hit.
- **D-11 Two variants, B must always work:**
  - **Variant A (wish, DEFERRED-ANALYST):** the drone video is uploaded to Hudl like game film and cut per play by the analyst → Hudl clip boundaries are the anchors, no manual marking. Documented with reasoning as a request.
  - **Variant B (fallback, always available):** raw video file outside Hudl; the user marks each snap manually → one anchor per play (never per drive — robust against unfilmed plays, battery gaps, penalties). No reliable timestamp is expected inside the drone material, so anchors are frame numbers / seconds offsets within a named video file. Phase 2.3 snap detection may later propose these anchors automatically; the convention must stay compatible with that.

### Legal & drone setup
- **D-12 EU-Drohnenverordnung: responsibility lies entirely with the operator** (staff/analyst). `docs/capture-legal.md` records this explicitly — the user neither checks nor collects category/registration/insurance evidence; at most a short orientation pointer. No operator checklist sign-off is required for the phase to complete.
- **D-13 DSGVO: consent is held by the federation and has been given by every person.** The legal note documents this as the basis for analysis use (reference to the federation's declaration, internal-only/analysis-only use of footage). Nothing new is drafted or collected.

### Claude's Discretion
- Exact filenames of the four docs and the inventory CSV; column set/order of the CSV beyond the minimum in D-07.
- Machine-readable anchor format for sync variant B (a CSV under `data/reference/` such as `video_sync.csv` with `game_id`, `play_id`, `video_file`, `snap_frame`/`snap_seconds`, optional end is the natural fit; YAML sidecar acceptable if better argued) — must be joinable with `data/processed/plays.parquet` on `game_id` + `play_id`.
- Where sample clips are stored locally and how the `.gitignore` is extended.
- Concrete numeric boundaries between Ideal / Brauchbar / Unbrauchbar tiers (derive from docs/research-notes.md evidence: TeamTrack top-down vs oblique, small-object regime, SAHI).
- Wording and structure of the Wunschzettel within the tone constraint of D-03; length ≈ one page for the drone part.
- How the DEFERRED-ANALYST blocks are phrased (mirror `docs/data-contract.md` §8).

</decisions>

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- "Ich bin nur der Datentyp und muss mit dem arbeiten, was kommt" — the user has no standing to prescribe; every document is a polite, reasoned request. Keep that tone in German.
- Tiers exist so the user can triage incoming footage in minutes, not to negotiate with staff.
- For sync, "alles, was in Hudl schon ist" is the anchor: reuse Hudl's per-play structure rather than inventing a parallel one; Hudl upload of drone film is the dream path, manual snap marks the guaranteed path.
- The user consistently prefers low-friction, honest documentation over process: no sign-offs, no consent forms, no operator checklists — document who is responsible and move on.

</specifics>
